PRIORITIZED CHANGES:
Replace the vector illustration with a moody, atmospheric photographic treatment or a textured mixed-media approach. Use a long-exposure or foggy aesthetic to create a sense of depth and 'fading' memory, rather than sharp geometric edges. Introduce a subtle color temperature shift—perhaps a cold, desaturated blue for the foreground and a faint, warm, dying light in the distance—to create emotional contrast. Increase the contrast of the title typography (perhaps adding a subtle glow or drop shadow) to ensure legibility against the dark background. The houses should feel like looming memories—blurred, translucent, or overgrown—rather than solid, flat shapes.
PROTECT:
The conceptual layout of the road acting as a leading line toward a solitary figure is strong and should be retained. The choice of a serif typeface is appropriate for the genre and should be kept, though its integration needs refinement. The minimalist approach to the narrative (showing the road, not the story) is the correct direction.
TRADE-OFF:
Prioritize atmospheric depth and texture over graphic clarity. The design must sacrifice the 'clean' vector look to gain the 'gritty' or 'dreamlike' quality necessary for a film about disappearing places. Legibility of the text must be secured through contrast adjustments, even if it means darkening the sky further or lightening the text.
