PRIORITIZED CHANGES:
Replace the low-poly vector shapes with a textured, atmospheric image or a painterly illustration that suggests a physical, decaying coastal town—use grain, fog, or light leaks to evoke memory. Shift the composition to a rule-of-thirds layout: place the road and figure slightly off-center to create visual tension. Increase the scale of the title 'WHERE THE HOUSES END' significantly, perhaps allowing the text to interact with the horizon line or the fading houses. Introduce a subtle color accent (like a pale, sickly yellow or a deep bruised purple) to break the monochromatic sterility and add emotional weight.
PROTECT:
The core metaphor of the solitary figure on a road leading into fading silhouettes is strong and should be kept. The title choice and the tagline 'Some places disappear twice' are excellent and capture the brief perfectly.
TRADE-OFF:
Prioritize atmospheric texture and emotional resonance over clean, geometric minimalism. The design must sacrifice 'tidiness' to gain the feeling of a lived-in, disappearing world.
