PRIORITIZED CHANGES:
1. **Visual Style Shift:** Abandon the geometric/low-poly aesthetic. Replace the vector-style houses with a photographic or painterly treatment that emphasizes texture, decay, and atmosphere. The houses should feel like physical structures dissolving into mist, not flat shapes. 2. **Typography Correction:** Remove the blur effect from the title. Use a crisp, elegant serif font (like Bodoni or Caslon) in a cool white or pale grey. The 'fading' effect should be achieved through opacity or integration with the background mist, not by blurring the pixels. 3. **Atmosphere & Lighting:** Introduce a subtle light source (moonlight or streetlamp glow) to create depth and contrast, moving away from the flat, dark void. The road should feel like asphalt or gravel, not a smooth polygon. 4. **Composition:** Break the rigid symmetry. Allow the houses to encroach slightly more on the frame to create a sense of claustrophobia or being watched by the past.
PROTECT:
The core metaphor of the road leading to a vanishing point and the solitary figure is strong and should be retained. The color palette of deep, cold blues is appropriate for the 'winter' and 'disappearing' themes and provides a good foundation for the mood.
TRADE-OFF:
We are trading the 'clean' and 'modern' look of the geometric style for a 'grittier' and more organic texture. This sacrifices the immediate visual clarity of the shapes in favor of emotional resonance and cinematic realism, which is essential for a film about memory and loss.
