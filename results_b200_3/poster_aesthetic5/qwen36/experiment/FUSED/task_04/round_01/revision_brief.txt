PRIORITIZED CHANGES:
Abandon the neon glow and waveform graphics in favor of a high-contrast, gritty aesthetic (e.g., grain, halftone, or aggressive ink bleed) to convey 'noise' and texture. Reconstruct the layout to be asymmetrical and dynamic: use the headline 'BLUE HOUR RED NOISE' as a structural element that breaks the grid, perhaps overlapping the performer list or tilting to create kinetic energy. Replace the rigid bottom grid with a typographic hierarchy that reflects the music—perhaps staggering the performer names or varying the type weights to mimic rhythm. The background should be an abstract, moody texture rather than a clean geometric overlay.
PROTECT:
The core color identity of Cyan vs. Red is effective and should be retained, but the application needs to move from 'glowing light' to 'pigment and ink' to feel more tactile and artistic. The legibility of the performer names is currently good and must be maintained.
TRADE-OFF:
Prioritize 'atmospheric texture' over 'digital clarity.' The design must sacrifice the clean, vector-perfect look of the current render to gain the raw, unpredictable energy required for an experimental jazz festival.
