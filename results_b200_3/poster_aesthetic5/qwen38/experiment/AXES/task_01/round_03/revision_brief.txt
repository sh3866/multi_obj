PRIORITIZED PANEL CHANGES (weigh all four equally):
1. Visual Concept & Imagery — CHANGE: 1. Replace the jagged lightning line with a smooth, curved trajectory or a simple dotted path to represent a falling ember or spark, removing the implication of a lightning strike. 2. Integrate the fire more directly into the forest silhouette by having the flames consume the nearest row of trees or by adding a subtle red/orange glow to the tree tips closest to the fire to visualize the immediate threat to the landscape. PROTECT: The strong vertical composition that guides the eye from the headline 'ONE SPARK IS ENOUGH' directly down to the source of the fire is excellent and must be maintained. The high-contrast color palette (dark blues/browns against bright orange/white) effectively creates the required urgency without being chaotic.
2. Composition & Spatial Hierarchy — CHANGE: 1. Reduce the opacity of the smoke plume in the upper third (where the text sits) by 30-40% or add a subtle dark radial gradient behind the headline to ensure the text 'pops' against the background. 2. Introduce a subtle, warm glow (a low-opacity orange radial gradient) at the base of the foreground tree silhouettes to visually connect the fire's energy to the bottom of the frame, creating a more unified vertical rhythm. PROTECT: The dramatic scale of the smoke plume relative to the small spark is the core narrative device; it must remain the dominant vertical element to convey the 'one spark' concept effectively.
3. Typography & Message Integration — CHANGE: Increase the font weight of the headline 'ONE SPARK IS ENOUGH' to a bold or heavy weight to give it visual authority and urgency. Simultaneously, reduce the weight of the subtext 'Check conditions...' to a regular or medium weight to establish a clear hierarchy where the message dominates and the instructions support it. PROTECT: The wide letter-spacing (tracking) on the headline and footer is a strong design choice that creates a sense of scale and atmosphere; this spacing should be maintained even as the weights are adjusted.
4. Color, Atmosphere & Craft — CHANGE: 1. Increase the tonal separation between the mountain layers by adding a subtle, cool-toned rim light (e.g., a desaturated blue or purple) to the top edges of the furthest mountain range, and a warmer, more saturated brown to the closer ranges. 2. Add a soft, radial gradient of warm orange light to the ground directly beneath and around the fire to create a stronger sense of illumination and ground the light source in the scene. PROTECT: The atmospheric quality of the smoke and the overall dark, urgent mood of the poster are excellent and must be preserved. The color harmony between the cool sky and warm fire is a key strength of the composition.
