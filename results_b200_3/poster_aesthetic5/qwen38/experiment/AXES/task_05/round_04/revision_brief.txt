PRIORITIZED PANEL CHANGES (weigh all four equally):
1. Visual Concept & Imagery — CHANGE: 1. Distort the road: Instead of straight, converging lines, make the road edges slightly wavy or broken, as if the asphalt is crumbling or the memory of the road is fading. 2. Fragment the houses: Break up the solid silhouettes of the houses. Have some roofs missing, walls partially transparent, or structures dissolving into the mist, visually reinforcing the 'disappearing' theme. PROTECT: The atmospheric color palette (deep blues, greys, and the stark white of the light source) and the effective use of the horizontal mist band to separate the 'real' foreground from the 'memory' background are strong, cinematic elements that must be preserved.
2. Composition & Spatial Hierarchy — CHANGE: 1. Tighten the letter-spacing of the main title to increase its visual weight and ensure it reads as a single, dominant block of text rather than a scattered line. 2. Increase the vertical spacing between the tagline and the credits, and slightly increase the font size of 'A film by Mara Venn' to create a clearer secondary hierarchy in the lower third. PROTECT: The atmospheric use of negative space in the upper half and the effective use of the road's perspective lines to draw the eye toward the central, solitary figure must be preserved.
3. Typography & Message Integration — CHANGE: 1. Unify the title 'WHERE THE HOUSES END' into a single, consistent color (a muted, desaturated grey-blue) and weight to restore legibility and allow the moon to remain the primary focal point of the upper section. 2. Increase the opacity and size of the bottom credit block ('A film by Mara Venn') by 20-30% to ensure it is legible without disrupting the dark, cinematic mood of the foreground. PROTECT: The extensive letter-spacing (tracking) on the main title is a strong choice that effectively conveys the themes of 'distance' and 'disappearing' mentioned in the brief. This airy, spaced-out quality must be preserved as it is the primary typographic driver of the poster's emotional tone.
4. Color, Atmosphere & Craft — CHANGE: Replace the hard-edged white horizon band with a soft, wide Gaussian blur or a radial gradient that fades gently into the surrounding dark tones. The light should feel like it is emanating from the distance or settling as mist, rather than being a distinct geometric shape. Additionally, slightly increase the opacity of the house silhouettes to ensure they remain legible against the bright background without losing their ghostly quality. PROTECT: The overall color temperature and the specific choice of a muted, desaturated blue-grey palette are excellent. The placement of the moon and the subtle, sparse bird silhouettes create a perfect sense of isolation and scale that must be preserved.
