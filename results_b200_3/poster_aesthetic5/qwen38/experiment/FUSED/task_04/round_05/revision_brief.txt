PRIORITIZED CHANGES:
1. Deconstruct the layout: Abandon the rigid center-alignment. Introduce asymmetry and tension by shifting the headline off-center or breaking the grid, allowing the 'noise' to disrupt the order. 2. Elevate the visual metaphor: Replace the literal waveform with a more abstract, dynamic representation of sound—perhaps overlapping, chaotic brushstrokes, fragmented geometric shapes, or a high-contrast, sharp-edged graphic that suggests 'interruption' and 'syncopation' rather than just a frequency line. 3. Enhance atmosphere: Clean up the background. Use sharper, more deliberate lighting effects or textures (like grain or halftone patterns) to create depth and a 'late-night' mood, rather than blurry, indistinct blobs. 4. Integrate typography: Make the type more expressive. Consider breaking the letters, overlapping them with the graphic elements, or varying the scale and weight more dramatically to create a visual rhythm that mirrors the music.
PROTECT:
The color palette (deep blue, bright red, and black) is strong and effectively establishes the 'Blue Hour / Red Noise' duality. The distressed texture on the main headline is a good starting point for conveying a raw, experimental feel.
TRADE-OFF:
The revision prioritizes dynamic, abstract visual energy and asymmetrical composition over the current design's safe, centered legibility. This trades some immediate 'at-a-glance' simplicity for a more distinctive, high-impact identity that better fits the 'experimental' brief.
