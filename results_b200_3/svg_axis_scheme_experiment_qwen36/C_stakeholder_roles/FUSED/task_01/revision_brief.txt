PRIORITIZED CHANGES:
1. **Grid Overhaul:** Replace the individual artist cards with a clean, continuous timeline grid. Use horizontal lines to separate time slots and vertical lines to separate stages. This reduces cognitive load and improves scanability.
2. **Visual Rhythm:** Use the 'glow' concept to differentiate genres. Instead of just text, apply subtle background tints or glowing borders to the specific time slots based on genre (e.g., a warm glow for Indie Rock, a cool cyan glow for Synthwave). This fulfills the 'meaningful connection' requirement without fragmenting the layout.
3. **Headliner Integration:** Instead of a separate footer banner, make the 7:00 PM slot the visual climax of the grid. Expand the typography size significantly and use a high-contrast gradient background for that specific row, making it feel like the 'main event' within the schedule structure rather than an add-on.
4. **Typography:** Upgrade the font hierarchy. Use a bold, condensed sans-serif for the stage headers and artist names to create a more 'contemporary festival' feel. Increase the size of the time column to make the chronological flow the primary navigation tool.
PROTECT:
The color-coded stage headers (Sunset/Neon/Moonlight) are effective for quick orientation and should be retained, though the colors can be refined to be more neon/expressive. The overall dark mode aesthetic is appropriate for the 'Afterglow' theme.
TRADE-OFF:
We are trading the 'card' aesthetic (which feels like a modern app interface) for a 'poster' aesthetic (which is better for reading a schedule). We are sacrificing the visual isolation of individual artists to gain the ability to scan the entire day's flow at a glance.
