- Completeness & Information Density: Defend direct visibility of requested information and useful detail. Do not accept cleanliness obtained by hiding, deleting, or over-compressing substantive content.

ORIGINAL BRIEF:
Design a polished single-page homepage for a fictional literary and cultural magazine called "Margin & Field," Issue No. 12: "The Shape of Distance." The page must visibly include the magazine name, issue number and theme; one dominant feature story with title, author, category and short excerpt; four secondary articles with title, author and category; a compact contents or issue summary; a prominent Read the Issue action; and a secondary subscription action. The page should feel editorial, thoughtful and culturally distinctive. Use expressive typography, image-like abstract forms created with CSS or inline SVG, and a sophisticated composition that reflects the theme of distance. At the same time, the reading order must be unmistakable. The feature story should dominate, while all four secondary articles remain meaningfully visible and readable rather than being reduced to tiny metadata. All stories must belong to one coherent editorial system but should not feel mechanically identical; their visual differences should arise from content importance, category and editorial meaning rather than arbitrary decoration. Balance a disciplined editorial grid with expressive composition, strong feature hierarchy with adequate visibility for secondary stories, typographic consistency with meaningful article variation, and generous negative space with complete and balanced use of the viewport. Do not achieve elegance by omitting requested content, and do not create variety by assigning an unrelated style to every article.
