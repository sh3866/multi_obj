PRIORITIZED CHANGES:
1. **Typography & Identity:** Replace standard fonts with a high-contrast pairing: a bold, condensed display font for the title and artist names (to create energy) and a clean geometric sans-serif for data. 2. **Schedule System:** Remove the heavy 'card' backgrounds for individual artists. Switch to a clean grid system where the Stage Columns are defined by subtle vertical lines or background tinting, and artists are listed as text blocks. This improves legibility and density. 3. **Headliner Integration:** Instead of a separate footer banner, integrate the headliner into the grid at the 7:00 PM slot. Make the text massive and spanning all three columns, or use a distinct background color for that specific row to signal the climax without breaking the grid structure. 4. **Visual Rhythm:** Use the genre colors (Orange, Green, Blue) as small accent elements (like a vertical bar or dot) next to the artist names rather than overwhelming stage headers, creating a more unified and modern look.
PROTECT:
The three-column stage layout is logically sound and should be kept. The color-coding of the stages (Orange/Green/Blue) is a strong functional tool for quick identification and should be retained, though refined.
TRADE-OFF:
Prioritizing 'System Consistency' and 'Legibility' over the current 'Card' aesthetic. We are sacrificing the 'chunky' look of the current design to gain a more professional, scannable, and typographically expressive schedule that feels like a cohesive festival identity rather than a UI wireframe.
