PRIORITIZED PANEL CHANGES (weigh all four equally):
1. Visual Concept & Imagery — CHANGE: Replace the hard-edged horizontal fog band with a more organic, vertical gradient that rises from the ground, allowing the bases of the houses and the streetlamp to dissolve into the mist more naturally. Additionally, slightly reduce the opacity of the houses on the far left and right edges compared to the center to create a sense of the town 'fading' at its periphery, reinforcing the title 'Where the Houses End'. PROTECT: The one-point perspective of the empty road leading to the solitary figure is the poster's strongest asset. It perfectly captures the 'distance' and 'uncertainty' of the brief. The scale difference between the tiny human and the looming, misty town is crucial and must be preserved.
2. Composition & Spatial Hierarchy — CHANGE: Reduce the font size of the main title by approximately 15-20% and increase the letter spacing to allow it to breathe within the sky, reducing its visual weight. Simultaneously, lower the opacity of the bottom text block (credits/tagline) to 60-70% to push it further into the background, ensuring the central road and figure remain the primary focal point of the composition. PROTECT: The atmospheric use of negative space in the sky and the effective use of the road's perspective lines to draw the eye toward the center must be preserved. The color palette's restraint is a key strength that should not be altered.
3. Typography & Message Integration — CHANGE: 1. Introduce a typographic hierarchy within the headline by increasing the tracking or slightly reducing the opacity of 'WHERE THE' and 'END' compared to 'THE HOUSES,' or by setting 'END' in a lighter weight to visually suggest a trailing off. 2. Apply a subtle vertical gradient mask to the headline text so the bottom of the letters fade slightly into the sky, allowing the type to feel like it is emerging from or dissolving into the atmosphere rather than being pasted on top. PROTECT: The elegant, wide tracking of the serif font and the restrained, low-contrast treatment of the lower credits must be preserved, as they successfully establish the cinematic and emotionally quiet tone required by the brief.
4. Color, Atmosphere & Craft — CHANGE: Reduce the opacity of the central white fog band by approximately 30-40% and apply a subtle vertical gradient to it, making it slightly more transparent at the top and bottom edges to blend more organically into the darker tones of the sky and the road. This will soften the horizon line and restore the atmospheric depth. PROTECT: The restrained, melancholic color palette and the effective use of negative space and haze to convey the theme of a 'disappearing' place are the core emotional strengths of the design and must be preserved.
