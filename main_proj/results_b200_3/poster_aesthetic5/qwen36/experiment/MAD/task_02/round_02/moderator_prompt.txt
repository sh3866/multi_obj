- Visual Concept & Imagery: Defend the strength of the poster's central visual idea and image direction. - Composition & Spatial Hierarchy: Defend the poster as a strong spatial composition. Evaluate focal dominance, scale relationships, balance, tension, negative space, alignment, rhythm, and the viewer's visual path. The poster should read as a deliberate whole from a distance and reveal secondary information upon closer inspection. Reject layouts that are merely tidy, centered, or evenly distributed without compositional intent.
- Typography & Message Integration: Defend typography as an expressive visual material, not merely readable text. Evaluate type choice, scale, weight, spacing, line breaks, hierarchy, and the integration of words with imagery. The headline should embody the intended voice and participate in the composition. Reject generic text overlays and decorative typography that weakens comprehension.
- Color, Atmosphere & Craft: Defend the poster's emotional atmosphere and production-level finish. Evaluate palette, tonal contrast, color relationships, texture, depth, lighting, graphical consistency, and fine execution. Color and surface treatment should reinforce the concept and hierarchy. Reject both sterile default styling and gratuitous effects that are not integrated into the visual system.

ORIGINAL BRIEF:
Create an expressive promotional poster for a contemporary dance performance about the tension between physical control and emotional release. Develop a distinctive central visual suggesting a human body in motion, balance, collapse, repetition, or restrained energy. The figure does not need to be anatomically realistic; silhouette, fragmentation, gesture, line, shadow, or abstraction may be used if they create a convincing sense of movement. Required text: THE WEIGHT OF AIR; A contemporary movement performance; October 18–20 · 8 PM; Meridian Arts Theatre. The poster should feel sophisticated, physical, and emotionally charged. Avoid generic luxury branding, conventional event-card layouts, and decorative motion effects that have no relationship to the performance. Do not design a schedule, webpage, collection of cards, or informational interface. Create one unified poster composition.
