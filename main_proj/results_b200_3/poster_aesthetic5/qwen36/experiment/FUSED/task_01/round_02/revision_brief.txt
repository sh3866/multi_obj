PRIORITIZED CHANGES:
Replace the abstract neon line with a visual metaphor that bridges the 'spark' and the 'mountain' more organically. Use a high-contrast, realistic or painterly style for the mountain to ground the poster in reality. Instead of a zig-zag, depict the fire as a creeping, organic line of flame or a trail of smoke that starts from a single, hyper-realistic spark (like a glowing ember or cigarette tip) and threatens to consume the peak. The headline should be integrated into the negative space of the sky, perhaps using a typeface with slightly rougher edges to match the texture of the fire. The color palette should shift from the current sterile blue to a more dramatic contrast of deep charcoal shadows and intense, warm amber/orange highlights to create a visceral sense of heat and danger.
PROTECT:
The core concept of the 'single spark' initiating a massive chain reaction is strong and should be retained. The diagonal composition creates necessary tension and movement, which is effective for a warning poster. The hierarchy of the text (Headline > Action > Brand) is logically sound.
TRADE-OFF:
The revision prioritizes emotional immediacy and environmental realism over the current 'clean/digital' aesthetic. We are trading the safe, abstract look for a more visceral, high-contrast approach that risks being darker but gains significantly in persuasive power and relevance to the subject matter.
