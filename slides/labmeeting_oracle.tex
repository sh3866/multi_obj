\date{2026.08.02}

% ================================================== 1. Motivation
\begin{frame}{Motivation: the reward is not a scalar}
\small
LLM agents now \emph{generate artifacts} (web pages, UIs, games), not just text.
The standard recipe is a \hl{generator--evaluator loop}: draft $\rightarrow$ critique $\rightarrow$ revise.
\cit{Anthropic harness; Self-Refine, Madaan+ '23; Reflexion, Shinn+ '23}

\vspace{0.35em}
\begin{block}{The problem}
For artifacts, ``quality'' is \hl{multi-objective and partly subjective}:
functionality, layout, color/typography, originality, craft.
These \bad{trade off against each other} --- adding visual richness costs whitespace;
enforcing a grid costs character.
\end{block}

\vspace{0.2em}
A \hl{single fused evaluator} collapses this into one scalar and one revision instruction.
Two known failure modes:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.15em
  \item \bad{Leniency drift} --- a self/single evaluator declares its own work good and stops improving.
        \cit{LLMs cannot self-correct reasoning yet, Huang+ ICLR'24}
  \item \bad{Objective collapse} --- one axis dominates the instruction; the others silently decay.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.35em}
\centering
\textbf{RQ:} \emph{Does giving each objective its own critic --- and letting them argue ---
produce better artifacts than one fused evaluator?}
\end{frame}

% ================================================== 2. Related work
\begin{frame}{Where this sits}
\scriptsize
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
\textbf{\color{navymain}Iterative self-improvement}\\
Self-Refine \cit{Madaan+ '23}, Reflexion \cit{Shinn+ '23}, CRITIC \cit{Gou+ '23}\\
$\rightarrow$ \emph{single} evaluator; gains shrink or reverse without external signal
\cit{Huang+ '24; Stechly+ '23}

\vspace{0.6em}
\textbf{\color{navymain}Multi-agent debate}\\
Du+ '23, Liang+ '23 --- debate improves factuality/reasoning\\
$\rightarrow$ evaluated on tasks with \bad{verifiable answers}.
Nobody tests debate where the objective is \emph{subjective and conflicting}.

\vspace{0.6em}
\textbf{\color{navymain}Fine-grained reward}\\
Fine-grained RLHF \cit{Wu+ '23}, Constitutional AI \cit{Bai+ '22}\\
$\rightarrow$ decomposition helps \emph{at training time}; we ask whether it helps
\hl{at inference time, inside the loop}.
\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
\textbf{\color{navymain}LLM-as-judge}\\
MT-Bench \cit{Zheng+ '23}, G-Eval \cit{Liu+ '23}\\
Known biases: position, verbosity, \bad{self-preference} \cit{Panickssery+ '24}\\
$\rightarrow$ forces our judge to be \emph{held out} + blind.

\vspace{0.6em}
\textbf{\color{navymain}Artifact benchmarks}\\
ArtifactsBench \cit{Tencent Hunyuan '25} --- MLLM judge over code + screenshots;
design is $\sim$1 checklist item out of 10\\
UI-Bench \cit{'25} --- human expert \emph{pairwise} + TrueSkill, pure aesthetics\\
$\rightarrow$ either design is a footnote, or evaluation needs humans.

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{block}{\scriptsize Gap}
\scriptsize Debate is validated on \emph{verifiable} tasks.
Artifact generation is \emph{subjective + multi-objective}.
We test the transfer directly, with a pre-registered protocol.
\end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

% ================================================== 3. Method
\begin{frame}{Method: an ablation ladder over the evaluator}
\scriptsize
Everything is held fixed except \hl{the structure of the evaluator}.

\vspace{0.3em}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{5.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{arm} & \textbf{evaluator} & \textbf{isolates} \\
\midrule
ZS    & none (one pass)                  & floor anchor \\
SELF  & generator critiques itself       & value of \emph{any} external evaluator \\
FUSED & one fused critic (harness)       & \hl{H1 control} \\
AXES  & 4 per-axis critics $\rightarrow$ moderator & \hl{H1}: does decomposition help? \\
MAD   & AXES + cross-critique (debate)   & \hl{H2}: does arguing help? \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.4em}
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\begin{column}{0.53\textwidth}
\textbf{\color{navymain}Controls (pre-registered)}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.1em
  \item \hl{Shared $r_0$}: all arms start from the \emph{same} draft
        (common random numbers) --- removes the draw lottery
  \item \hl{Budget-matched}: equal token budget, not equal rounds
  \item \hl{Consensus-stop}: each arm stops when \emph{its own}
        evaluator says ``good enough'' $\Rightarrow$ leniency is measurable
  \item Critics emit \emph{critique only}; the generator rewrites the code
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.45\textwidth}
\textbf{\color{navymain}Evaluation (3-layer isolation)}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.1em
  \item In-loop critics $\neq$ held-out judge
  \item Judge sees a \hl{temporal screenshot series} (load / settled / after-click)
  \item \hl{Blind}: variants anonymised to codes, shuffled, neutral paths;
        un-mapped only after scoring
  \item 12 ArtifactsBench tasks; axes = functionality / design / originality / craft
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

% ================================================== 4. Result 1
\begin{frame}{Result 1: decomposition + debate helps --- when the axes really conflict}
\scriptsize
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
\textbf{\color{navymain}(a) Main run} --- 12 tasks, shared $r_0$, cap 10 rounds, blind judge
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
arm & overall (0--10) \\
\midrule
$r_0$ (shared draft) & 4.58 \\
SELF                 & 5.34 \\
FUSED (harness)      & 5.35 \\
AXES                 & \gd{5.92} \\
MAD                  & \gd{5.97} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.2em}
$\rightarrow$ \hl{+0.6 for decomposition} over a fused evaluator.\\
Debate $\approx$ independent axes at the \emph{final} artifact.

\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{\color{navymain}Round trajectory} (every round judged blind)\\
SELF / FUSED peak early (r3--6) then plateau;
AXES / MAD keep climbing ($\sim$+1.5) --- consistent with
\hl{peer critique suppressing leniency drift}.
\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
\textbf{\color{navymain}(b) Is the critic the bottleneck?}\\
Same structure, same shared $r_0$, one round ---
only the \emph{critic text} is swapped (in-house MoE $\rightarrow$ hand-written Opus ``gold'').
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
structure & weak & gold & $\Delta$ \\
\midrule
fused & 4.38 & 4.75 & +0.38 \\
axes  & 4.25 & \gd{5.27} & \gd{+1.02} \\
mad   & 4.23 & 4.44 & +0.21 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.3em}
$\rightarrow$ critic \emph{quality} matters, and it pays off most
when the structure can \hl{route it per axis}.

\vspace{0.5em}
\begin{block}{\scriptsize Diagnostic found along the way}
\scriptsize A VLM parser bug silently dropped \bad{86\%} of axis critiques
(``no parse''). After the fix: \gd{100\%} parse (960/960).
The earlier ``debate helps'' signal survived re-running.
\end{block}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

% ================================================== 5. Result 2 + takeaway
\begin{frame}{Result 2: strip out functionality, and the effect disappears}
\scriptsize
The main tasks still reward \emph{functionality}. So: rewrite all 12 briefs as
\hl{single-screen, design-only} tasks, make critics \hl{score-free} (pure critique),
and vary axis granularity (2 / 4 / 10).

\vspace{0.35em}
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\textbf{\color{navymain}(a) Design-only: null result}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
arm ($\times$ granularity) & score \\
\midrule
FUSED-10 / FUSED-2 & 6.42 / 6.41 \\
MAD-10             & 6.25 \\
\bad{$r_0$ (no refinement)} & \bad{5.96} \\
AXES (all), SELF, MAD-4 & 5.79--5.96 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All arms sit within $\pm$0.5 of the untouched draft; \hl{$r_0$ is mid-pack}.
Granularity is not monotone. 18\% of finals score $<$4.5 --- \bad{render breakage
dominates the variance}, not taste.
\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.51\textwidth}
\textbf{\color{navymain}(b) Oracle ceiling} --- is the critic even the bottleneck?\\
Give the loop a critic \emph{perfectly aligned with the judge}:
same 4 axes, same 0--10 anchors, plus ``here is what a 10/10 looks like for this brief''.
Generator = Haiku, 3 rounds, 96 blind per-axis judgments.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
 & $r_0$ & $r_1$ & $r_2$ & $r_3$ \\
\midrule
mean & 5.00 & \gd{5.77} & 5.21 & 5.10 \\
spacing axis & 5.82 & 5.58 & 4.65 & \bad{4.50} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.2em}
$\rightarrow$ \gd{+0.77 at round 1}, then \bad{collapse}. Net $+0.11$.
4/6 tasks peak at $r_1$. Ceiling $\approx$7, never near 9--10.
Failure mode: the generator keeps \emph{adding} what the critique asks for
until the layout breaks.
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\vspace{0.4em}
\begin{block}{\scriptsize Takeaway}
\scriptsize
Decomposition + debate helps \hl{when objectives genuinely conflict and part of the signal is verifiable}.
On \emph{pure} subjective design it buys nothing --- and an \hl{oracle critic proves the bottleneck is
not critique quality} but the generator's ability to absorb long instructions without regression.
\bad{Next}: regression-aware critics (``preserve what works''), render gating, and pairwise
(not absolute) evaluation.
\end{block}
\end{frame}
