[Brief & Intent Alignment | brief_intent]
CRITIQUE:
The screen visibly establishes a grid-based industrial puzzle with a title, level and move HUD, controls legend, board walls, a player, two crates, two marked floor objectives, a red-handled lever, and an exit/door area. The board is the central artifact and the objective panel names both crate placement and door access. The critical alignment ambiguity is object identification and relationship: the lever is a tiny diagonal mark immediately beside the exit, the target tiles resemble hazard emblems rather than clearly matching crate destinations, and the door itself is only a very small EXIT label. As a result, pushing boxes and pulling a lever to open a door is not as visually self-explanatory as the brief requires.

SUGGESTION:
Preserve the complete board, player, two crates, two goals, controls, HUD, and objectives, but make the mechanic relationships explicit. Redesign the two targets to visually match crate footprints, enlarge and mechanically distinguish the lever with a base and handle state, and render a clearly recognizable closed door adjacent to it. Use a visible linkage cue—such as matching color, cable, or lock indicator—between the lever and door, while keeping the player and crates separate and readable within the grid.

[Clarity & Information Hierarchy | clarity_hierarchy]
CRITIQUE:
The puzzle board is centered and identifiable, but it occupies less than half the available width and is surrounded by large dark areas, so it does not achieve the expected centerpiece dominance. The title and level/move HUD are very small at the top, and the controls and objectives panels sit far from the board with weak visual connections to the player, crate, lever, goal, and door they describe. Within the dark grid, interactable states have similar scale and contrast, making the intended action sequence difficult to infer at a glance.

SUGGESTION:
Enlarge the board substantially and pull the controls and objectives closer to its left and right edges, creating one compact gameplay group rather than three distant islands. Increase the title and HUD enough to establish a clear top status tier. Use consistent callouts or matched symbols to connect the lever and door, crates and goals, and player movement controls without obscuring the grid. Establish the primary reading path as title/status → board state → current objective → controls, and reduce unused outer space accordingly.

[Visual System & Craft | visual_system_craft]
CRITIQUE:
The charcoal, oxidized gray, rust, brass, and cyan palette is disciplined, and the square tile grammar gives the board a sturdy industrial identity. The board still shows construction inconsistencies: floor tiles have inset gradients while wall tiles use diagonal stripes, yet both share uneven dark seams; object outlines and shadows vary widely between crates, player, goals, lever, and door. The lever is rendered with smooth rounded geometry while goals are highly detailed radial symbols and the player is a glossy ring, so interactables do not feel cut from one asset set. Very small low-contrast instructional copy and status lines reduce typographic finish, and the board's large black unused strip on the right reads as an untrimmed container.

SUGGESTION:
Trim the board container precisely to the 10-column grid and establish one tile-gap, border, and inset-shadow token. Redraw every interactable on a shared icon grid with consistent outline weight, highlight direction, shadow depth, and limited material rules—wood for crates/door, brass for targets/lever, cyan for player. Standardize sidebar card borders, heading tracking, body size, and status colors, increasing small-text contrast. Align both side modules and header HUD to the board's grid lines and remove residual empty container space so the screen feels intentionally fabricated rather than debug-layout adjacent.

[expressiveness | expressiveness]
CRITIQUE:
The industrial foundry palette, grid, crates, lever, and hazard targets establish a grounded puzzle identity, but the board tiles are highly repetitive and the player is an anonymous glowing dot. Objectives and controls use conventional utility panels without much authored worldbuilding.

SUGGESTION:
Give player, crates, lever, door, and targets distinct foundry-specific silhouettes and state changes, introduce purposeful floor wear and heat markings along likely routes, and create one expressive lever-to-door visual linkage while preserving board readability.
