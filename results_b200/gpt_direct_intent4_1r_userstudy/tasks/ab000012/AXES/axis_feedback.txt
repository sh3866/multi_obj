[Brief & Intent Alignment | brief_intent]
CRITIQUE:
The artifact strongly aligns with the requested Spirit Summoning hub: a visible title, atmospheric central spirit/veil hero, gold counter, and four crafted menu cards labeled Summon, Roster, Battle, and Grind are all present. The Summon card is emphasized and its subtitle connects it to the central ritual, so the hub's primary action is coherent. The main weakness is that the hero area's spirit is a very abstract faceted silhouette with no summoning circle, altar, caster, or manifestation cues beyond rings and copy; it can read as a decorative crystal rather than a summoned spirit. The other menu cards are dark enough that their distinct hub destinations are somewhat weakly instantiated.

SUGGESTION:
Keep the title, gold counter, four exact menu destinations, and centered mystical hero, but strengthen the summoning event with a luminous ritual seal or altar beneath the spirit, a clearer spectral face/body edge, and particles visibly emerging through the veil. Increase the legibility of Roster, Battle, and Grind subtitles/icons without competing with the active Summon card, so every requested hub destination remains substantively actionable rather than receding into decoration.

[Clarity & Information Hierarchy | clarity_hierarchy]
CRITIQUE:
The central spirit and title create a serene hero focus, and the highlighted Summon card clearly indicates the primary menu option. However, the hero content is relatively small within a very large empty middle field, while the four menu cards are compressed against the bottom edge; the eye pauses in unused space rather than flowing naturally from title to action. The gold indicator is isolated in the extreme upper-right corner with no header structure, and the secondary menu cards use very faint labels and descriptions that are difficult to scan.

SUGGESTION:
Scale the spirit/title group up modestly or move it lower, and raise the menu row so the hero and navigation form one continuous vertical sequence. Preserve atmospheric space around the spirit but remove the excessive gap before the cards. Increase the contrast and internal hierarchy of Roster, Battle, and Grind while keeping Summon clearly selected. Give the gold counter a small, aligned top utility band or anchor so it reads as persistent status rather than a floating badge.

[Visual System & Craft | visual_system_craft]
CRITIQUE:
The deep aubergine ground, lavender aura, restrained gold, and serif/sans pairing are cohesive, and the four menu cards share a clear construction system. The hero illustration is less resolved: concentric circle strokes vary between solid and dashed without material rationale, and the central spirit is a very flat faceted silhouette whose tiny eyes and abrupt lower points feel unfinished beside the elegant typography. Star texture is extremely faint and unevenly clipped. Menu-card secondary labels and eyebrow text are too low contrast, card accent glyphs use unrelated stroke/fill styles, and the selected card's purple surface is much flatter and brighter than the translucent dark cards. The header gold counter uses a different border density and corner treatment from the menu.

SUGGESTION:
Refine the hero as one coherent mystical illustration: use a consistent sigil stroke hierarchy, controlled glow falloff, and a more precisely shaded spirit silhouette with clean facial and lower-edge geometry. Establish shared tokens for panel border, translucent surface, selected state, gold accent, and shadow, then apply them to the currency counter and all menu cards. Redraw the four card glyphs with one size, stroke weight, and optical alignment; raise small-label contrast and standardize tracking. Make the selected card an elevated variant of the same material rather than a separate flat purple block, and cleanly bound the star texture within safe margins.

[expressiveness | expressiveness]
CRITIQUE:
The violet summoning circle, spirit silhouette, and dark menu create a clear mystical identity, but the four menu cards are nearly identical and the central spirit is a generic faceless shape. The screen feels polished yet restrained to the point that Summon, Roster, Battle, and Grind lack distinct fantasy roles.

SUGGESTION:
Make the summoned spirit and ritual seal more bespoke, differentiate each menu by a restrained symbolic material and motion cue tied to its function, and introduce one memorable ritual event—runes igniting, spectral fragments, or a portal depth effect—without fragmenting the unified violet system.
