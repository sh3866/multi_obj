[Brief & Intent Alignment | brief_intent]
CRITIQUE:
The page convincingly reads as a JS Planet Defense start screen. The game title, mission briefing, prominent Start Mission action, planet-threat illustration, HUD counters for wave/core HP/credits, and the complete Damage/Fire Rate/Speed upgrade row are all explicit and visible. The only substantive intent ambiguity is the state of the screen: showing Wave 07/20 and Core HP 92% suggests a match already in progress, while the dominant action and brief present a home/start state. The upgrade modules display levels but offer no visible purchase cost or affordance, so they read more like a loadout summary than menu buttons for choosing upgrades.

SUGGESTION:
Resolve the start-state contradiction by presenting the HUD as an upcoming mission forecast or resetting wave/HP to a pre-launch state. Make each Damage, Fire Rate, and Speed module unmistakably actionable with a purchase/select cue and credit cost while preserving their visible levels. Keep the Start Mission action dominant and retain the planet, briefing, and all three HUD metrics; these are the elements that make the requested defense-game home screen immediately recognizable.

[Clarity & Information Hierarchy | clarity_hierarchy]
CRITIQUE:
The giant planet and oversized title form two strong anchors, but the mission briefing and Start Mission button sit as separate blocks with a weak connective reading path between them. The planet's scale makes the scene immediately legible, yet it also pulls attention away from the action needed to begin. The three upgrades are compressed into a thin bottom strip with small labels and levels, making them read more like a status footer than actionable menu choices. Header counters are clear but visually far removed from the mission and loadout information they contextualize.

SUGGESTION:
Create a deliberate left-column sequence by grouping the title, mission brief, and Start Mission action more tightly, with the button directly following the briefing. Keep the planet as the visual hero but slightly reduce or shift it so the start flow has adequate prominence. Increase the height and internal hierarchy of the Damage, Fire Rate, and Speed cards, clearly separating name, current level, and upgrade affordance. Align the loadout heading with this row and visually relate the top HUD values to the corresponding mission/loadout regions without increasing overall clutter.

[Visual System & Craft | visual_system_craft]
CRITIQUE:
The screen sustains a strong dark-space palette with cyan, electric blue, magenta, and restrained yellow roles, and its angular panels and monospaced labels form a credible system. The planet is the main craft weakness: its soft, blurry green shapes and smooth gradient feel like an unfinished placeholder beside the precise linework, pixel stars, and sharp typography. Fine cyan orbital lines collide visually with the sphere edge and vary in opacity, while scattered star pixels are unevenly cropped around panels. The outlined second line of the title has fragile legibility, several micro-labels are too dim, and upgrade cards have slightly inconsistent accent frames and internal vertical alignment. The bottom card row sits extremely close to the viewport edge.

SUGGESTION:
Rebuild the planet with a deliberate surface vocabulary—clean vector landmasses, controlled atmospheric rim, consistent shadow falloff, and sharper orbital geometry—so it matches the UI's precision. Establish one stroke scale for orbits, panel outlines, dividers, and icon boxes; clip background particles cleanly behind content. Strengthen the outlined title fill or stroke for reliable contrast, raise secondary-text contrast, and place all three upgrade modules on one strict internal grid with identical icon, label, stat, and level baselines. Add a consistent bottom safe-area and unify card accent colors by semantic role rather than per-card decoration.

[expressiveness | expressiveness]
CRITIQUE:
The planet, orbital particles, and neon command framing create a strong defense-console identity, but the upgrade row and briefing panel remain standard HUD rectangles. Damage, fire rate, and speed are distinguished mainly by small labels, missing opportunities for semantically expressive controls.

SUGGESTION:
Give each upgrade a distinct functional icon and micro-visualization, make the mission threat visible through a content-specific enemy trajectory or shield stress cue, and develop the START control as a memorable launch interaction while retaining the disciplined command aesthetic.
